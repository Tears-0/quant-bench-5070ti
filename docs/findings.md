# Findings

Long-form analysis. Per-hypothesis verdicts with evidence.

> **Status: Tier A `smoke` complete — M1 (latency/memory/power) and M2 (quality, incl.
> downstream accuracy).** Every table between `quantbench` markers is generated by
> `quantbench report` from `results/`; prose numbers cite the record they come from. Nothing
> here is an estimate, and no hypothesis verdict is written ahead of its data (§0.1).

Snapshot: 2026-10-17/18, RTX 5070 Ti, driver 617.14, native Windows 11, llama.cpp b11706,
torch 2.11.0+cu128, transformers 5.20.1, lm-eval 0.4.13. Scope: one model (Qwen3-4B), two
GGUF quants (Q8_0, Q4_K_M) against BF16/F16 references.

---

## Summary

Tier A only, two quants — a demonstration of the instrument, not yet the recommendation:

1. **Q8_0 is indistinguishable from the reference** on every measure: KL 0.0016 nats/token,
   98% top-1 agreement, and all four downstream tasks tied (retention 99.8–100.5%).
2. **Q4_K_M costs measurable quality.** KL is 0.05 (31× Q8_0), the top choice changes at
   ~9% of positions, and two downstream losses are statistically real: GSM8K −2.0 points
   (95% CI −3.6 to −0.3) and HellaSwag −0.5 (−0.9 to −0.1). ARC and MMLU are tied.
3. **Q4_K_M is 39% faster at batch 1 and uses 38% less VRAM** than Q8_0 (199 vs 144
   decode tok/s; 2.8 vs 4.5 GiB). Across sessions on this desktop host, though, Q4_K_M's
   speed varied by ~8% (methodology §6.7).
4. **Perplexity moved proportionally less than KL** in both instruments (H2, preliminary),
   but by how much depends on the instrument; two configurations cannot carry a verdict.
5. The harness's own checks caught a dozen issues worth knowing about any such study on this
   stack, from llama.cpp's `--fit` default and floored `PPL(base)` to Windows' silent
   sysmem fallback (docs/failures.md).

### M2 quality results (Tier A, 2026-10-17)

Source records: `results/raw/qwen3-4b__gguf-q8-0__llama-cpp__b1__ctx512__gen0__quality__attn-cuda-fa.json`,
`…gguf-q4-k-m…quality…json`, and the reference runs
`qwen3-4b__gguf-f16__llama-cpp__reference__wikitext2.json` /
`qwen3-4b__bf16-reference__transformers__reference__wikitext2.json`.

| vs F16 GGUF, 40,920 positions (llama-perplexity, full vocabulary) | Q8_0 | Q4_K_M |
|---|---|---|
| KL mean, nats/token (± s.e.) | 0.00165 ± 0.00002 | 0.0509 ± 0.0009 |
| KL p90 / p99 / max | 0.0038 / 0.0145 / 0.31 | 0.112 / 0.527 / 19.2 |
| Top-1 agreement | 98.04% | 90.96% |
| PPL (F16 reference 10.6672, same positions) | 10.7183 (+0.051) | 10.7353 (+0.068) |
| Effective bpw, embedding-excluded | 8.50 | 4.78 |

**M2 acceptance: met.** Q8_0 is near-zero (0.0016 nats/token, top-1 98%); Q4_K_M is 31×
higher, separated by ~53 standard errors, on one verified token stream. The regression test
`tests/test_m2_acceptance.py`, whose band was written before the run, passes.

The Transformers BF16 reference over the full corpus (298,792 positions) has PPL 13.05. It
is **not** comparable with the 10.67 above: llama-perplexity scores only the second half of
each 2,048-token chunk, where every position has ≥ 1,024 tokens of context (§6.1).

### H1 — Q5_K_M vs Q4_K_M, and the KV-pressure inversion

> Q5_K_M is a strictly better quality-per-VRAM tradeoff than Q4_K_M for models ≤ 8B, but
> the ordering inverts once KV cache pressure at long context dominates.

**Evidence required:** paired KL and `vram_peak_nvsmi_mb` for both variants on Tiers A and
B, plus the context sweep at 512/2048/8192/32768 so the crossover — if any — is visible
rather than asserted. The relevant figures are 1 (Pareto), 4 (VRAM composition) and 8
(context cliff).

**Verdict:** _pending._

### H2 — Perplexity understates quantization damage

> Perplexity delta materially understates quantization damage compared to KL divergence;
> two quants with near-identical PPL can differ substantially in top-1 agreement.

**Evidence required:** figure 3 (PPL delta vs KL) plus the `top1_agreement` column. The
interesting region is the right-hand side of that plot: points with large KL and near-zero
PPL delta. A refutation looks like a tight monotone relationship between the two.

**Instrument check:** the M2 regression test asserts that a near-lossless perturbation
lands near zero KL while a 4-bit-scale perturbation is measurably worse, so a null result
here would be a fact about quantization, not a broken measurement.

**Preliminary evidence (n = 2 per instrument, Tier A, GGUF only).** The same two quants
were measured by two independent instruments (figure 3):

| Q8_0 → Q4_K_M | KL ratio | PPL-cost ratio | top-1 agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| llama.cpp, full-vocab KL vs F16 GGUF, 40,920 positions | 31× (0.0016 → 0.051) | 1.33× (+0.051 → +0.068) | 98.0% → 91.0% |
| Transformers, top-128 KL vs BF16, 298,792 positions | 51× (0.0011 → 0.058) | 8.4× (+0.023 → +0.194) | 98.2% → 90.7% |

Both instruments agree that **PPL moves proportionally less than KL**, which is the
direction H2 predicts. They disagree strongly about *how much*. An earlier draft of this
section, written from the llama.cpp numbers alone, called the PPL cost "only 1.33×"; the
second instrument shows that figure is instrument-dependent and should not be quoted on its
own. Part of the gap is plausibly llama.cpp-specific: its Q8_0 path quantizes activations to
q8_1 in its matmul kernels while the F16 base does not, which adds a PPL cost to Q8_0 that
the dequantized-weights Transformers path does not see. That is a hypothesis, not a finding.

What *is* robust is the downstream consequence. Q4_K_M's PPL cost is small in either
instrument (+0.6% to +1.5%), yet it produces a statistically real loss on GSM8K, the one
multi-step generative task: −2.0 points, CI −3.6 to −0.3 (see "Downstream accuracy").
Q8_0, with near-zero KL, loses nothing measurable anywhere. On this evidence KL and top-1
agreement track downstream damage at least as well as PPL does. The core profile adds Q6_K,
Q5_K_M, IQ4_XS and the imatrix pair on the same streams, which turns two points into a curve.

A methodological trap found on the way, relevant to anyone checking this with llama.cpp
alone: its own `PPL(Q) − PPL(base)` would have reported +0.118 and +0.135, because `PPL(base)`
is computed from floored stored log-probs (docs/failures.md).

**Verdict:** _pending — preliminary support for the direction, not the magnitude._

### H3 — Marlin-class INT4 vs GGUF, batched and at batch 1

> On `sm_120`, INT4 formats with Marlin-class kernels (AWQ/GPTQ via vLLM) beat GGUF Q4_K_M
> on batched throughput, but GGUF wins or ties at batch size 1 — the regime most local
> users actually run.

**Evidence required:** figure 5 (throughput vs batch size, log-x) across {1, 2, 4, 8, 16,
32}, with IQRs. Where IQRs overlap at batch 1 the answer is "tied", and that is a real
answer.

**Caveat that must be stated with any verdict:** if vLLM could not be built for `sm_120`
(fallback §12.1), the INT4 arm comes from Transformers + `GPTQModel` kernels instead, which
is a different kernel path and a weaker claim. `docs/failures.md` records which situation
applied.

**Verdict:** _pending._

### H4 — KV-cache quantization vs weight quantization, per unit of quality

> KV cache quantization buys more usable context per GB than weight quantization does, per
> unit of quality lost.

**Evidence required:** the Tier B KV sweep (`f16`/`q8_0`/`q4_0` on llama.cpp, `auto`/`fp8`
on vLLM) paired with quality runs at each KV dtype, against the weight-format ladder at
fixed context. Figures 4 and 8.

**Verdict:** _pending._

## Downstream accuracy (lm-eval)

Source records: `results/raw/qwen3-4b__{bf16-reference,gguf-q8-0,gguf-q4-k-m}__transformers__…lm-eval-tasks…json`;
per-item samples in `results/lm_eval/`; paired table in `results/lm_eval_paired.parquet`.

All three configurations answered the same 14,708 items with byte-identical prompts
(verified per item via lm-eval's `prompt_hash`), so each comparison is **paired**: per item,
did the quant score differently from the reference? This is why the intervals below are
about 2.5× tighter than the published per-score standard errors, and it is the only reason
HellaSwag's half-point loss is detectable at all.

* **Q8_0: no measurable loss anywhere.** Its per-item outcomes are almost the reference's:
  on HellaSwag, 22 items flip each way out of 10,042.
* **Q4_K_M: two real losses, both small.** GSM8K −2.0 points (73 items lost, 47 gained) and
  HellaSwag −0.5. ARC (−1.3) and the MMLU subset (−1.4) point the same way but their
  intervals include zero, so they are reported as tied, not as losses.
* GGUF quants were scored dequantized through Transformers in fp16, like the reference (Q15
  (a)): this measures the *weights*, not llama.cpp's kernels. Each dequantized checkpoint
  was first required to reproduce llama.cpp's own KL and top-1 agreement for that quant.

Runtime: ~95–108 minutes per configuration, ~75% of it GSM8K (no fused attention kernel on
Windows PyTorch, CPU-bound decode; open question Q16).

## Method notes worth repeating in any writeup

* KL divergence, not perplexity, is the primary quality metric — and the truncation used to
  make it tractable is documented, with its tail term, in
  [methodology.md](methodology.md#the-truncation-and-its-tail-term).
* Latency is derived from per-token arrival timestamps, so the ITL tail is measured.
* `vram_peak_nvsmi_mb` is the number that decides whether something fits; the allocator
  figure is smaller and would flatter every configuration equally.
* Runs are shuffled and temperature-gated, because otherwise format and thermal state are
  the same variable.
* Where quality and speed came from different backends, `scored_by_engine` says so.
