### H1 — Q5_K_M vs Q4_K_M, and the KV-pressure inversion

> Q5_K_M is a strictly better quality-per-VRAM tradeoff than Q4_K_M for models ≤ 8B, but
> the ordering inverts once KV cache pressure at long context dominates.

**Evidence required:** paired KL and `vram_peak_nvsmi_mb` for both variants on Tiers A and
B, plus the context sweep at 512/2048/8192/32768 so the crossover — if any — is visible
rather than asserted. The relevant figures are 1 (Pareto), 4 (VRAM composition) and 8
(context cliff).

**Verdict:** _pending._

### H2 — Perplexity understates quantization damage

> Perplexity delta materially understates quantization damage compared to KL divergence;
> two quants with near-identical PPL can differ substantially in top-1 agreement.

**Evidence required:** figure 3 (PPL delta vs KL) plus the `top1_agreement` column. The
interesting region is the right-hand side of that plot: points with large KL and near-zero
PPL delta. A refutation looks like a tight monotone relationship between the two.

**Instrument check:** the M2 regression test asserts that a near-lossless perturbation
lands near zero KL while a 4-bit-scale perturbation is measurably worse, so a null result
here would be a fact about quantization, not a broken measurement.

**Verdict:** _pending._

### H3 — Marlin-class INT4 vs GGUF, batched and at batch 1

> On `sm_120`, INT4 formats with Marlin-class kernels (AWQ/GPTQ via vLLM) beat GGUF Q4_K_M
> on batched throughput, but GGUF wins or ties at batch size 1 — the regime most local
> users actually run.

**Evidence required:** figure 5 (throughput vs batch size, log-x) across {1, 2, 4, 8, 16,
32}, with IQRs. Where IQRs overlap at batch 1 the answer is "tied", and that is a real
answer.

**Caveat that must be stated with any verdict:** if vLLM could not be built for `sm_120`
(fallback §12.1), the INT4 arm comes from Transformers + `GPTQModel` kernels instead, which
is a different kernel path and a weaker claim. `docs/failures.md` records which situation
applied.

**Verdict:** _pending._

### H4 — KV-cache quantization vs weight quantization, per unit of quality

> KV cache quantization buys more usable context per GB than weight quantization does, per
> unit of quality lost.

**Evidence required:** the Tier B KV sweep (`f16`/`q8_0`/`q4_0` on llama.cpp, `auto`/`fp8`
on vLLM) paired with quality runs at each KV dtype, against the weight-format ladder at
fixed context. Figures 4 and 8.

**Verdict:** _pending._

## Method notes worth repeating in any writeup

* KL divergence, not perplexity, is the primary quality metric — and the truncation used to
  make it tractable is documented, with its tail term, in
  [methodology.md](methodology.md#the-truncation-and-its-tail-term).
* Latency is derived from per-token arrival timestamps, so the ITL tail is measured.
* `vram_peak_nvsmi_mb` is the number that decides whether something fits; the allocator
  figure is smaller and would flatter every configuration equally.
* Runs are shuffled and temperature-gated, because otherwise format and thermal state are
  the same variable.
* Where quality and speed came from different backends, `scored_by_engine` says so.
